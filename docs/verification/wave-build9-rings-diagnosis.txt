Conclusion

No evidence-grounded product-source fix is established for the remaining rings failures. The new native
evidence shows an unnamed, childless XCTest Other node carrying wave.rings. It does NOT establish that

What is proved, and what is not
------------------------------
Proved: the identifier remains present in XCTest's descendant snapshot on the tested states despite the
candidate's explicit containing boundaries, ignored-child boundary and final accessibilityHidden(true).
The proposed Build 8 repair did not satisfy that unchanged strict absence predicate in Build 9.
There is no ring label or child text in these diagnostic nodes, and the approved meaningful invitation
state is still carried separately by the app's text.

Not proved: whether that Other node can receive VoiceOver focus, be spoken, or interfere with focus order;
whether XCTest preserves an intentionally hidden structural wrapper; or whether SwiftUI is exposing an
unwanted accessible element. Empty label/zero children do not prove inaccessibility, and snapshot existence
does not prove VoiceOver exposure. The property hasFocus=0 does not close that gap.

Apple's public contracts distinguish the two concepts: accessibilityHidden specifies hiding from system
accessibility features, while XCUIElementSnapshot/dictionaryRepresentation describe user-interface
attributes and descendant hierarchy. They do not promise that every snapshot descendant is a VoiceOver
stop or that every hidden decoration disappears from a diagnostic snapshot. Therefore the current test
could be over-strong for the intended decoration requirement. That is a supported possibility, not a
finding that licenses changing the contract or recording a pass.

Why no further source-only rings patch is proposed
-------------------------------------------------
Modifier reordering and explicit semantic containers have already been tried natively without removing
the node. Another ordering/container change, EmptyView accessibility representation, or disabling more
interaction would be speculative without evidence that it addresses the actual accessible hierarchy.
Removing/moving the identifier could hide the same structure from the predicate without repairing the
user experience; filtering Other nodes from the assertion could mask a real focus defect. Neither is
proposed. The current source and strict assertion are preserved for an interpretable next comparison.
